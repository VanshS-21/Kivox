## Anti-Patterns Verdict

The site does not look like a throwaway AI template. The parent Kivox surface has taste: light-first atmosphere, strong typography, polished work imagery, and a calm contact flow. The risk is not generic ugliness; it is trust confusion.

The AI-slop fingerprints are still visible: repeated bracketed mono kickers, equal service cards, nested card stacks, generic agency language, and concept worlds that do not consistently match their case studies. A skeptical SMB owner may feel the quality, but still wonder which names are clients, which are concepts, and which outcomes are real.

Deterministic scan:
- `npx impeccable detect --json --fast src` found 3 warnings: `bg-black` in `src/components/ui/ProjectCarousel.tsx:100`, and `font-family:Inter` in `src/features/inquiry/adapters/resendAdapter.ts:73` and `:119`.
- URL detection on `/`, `/work`, `/contact`, and `/showcase/medqueue` found repeated section kickers, nested cards, overuse of Geist Mono as visible text, all-caps text, and the MedQueue hero eyebrow chip.
- Live route checks found broken remote images in `/showcase/school` at `src/app/showcase/school/page.tsx:267` and `:274`, plus an unlabeled newsletter input in `/showcase/cafe` at `src/app/showcase/cafe/page.tsx:675`.

Official `npx impeccable live --port ...` did not serve `detect.js` in this environment, so no visual overlay tab is available. The URL detector and Playwright screenshots were used instead.

## Overall Impression

Kivox already has a believable studio voice. The visual system is capable of earning trust, especially on `/work`, `/contact`, and MedQueue. The biggest opportunity is to make the proof architecture as honest and polished as the interface: one clear story of what Kivox has built, what is a self-initiated concept, and why a business owner should believe it.

## What's Working

1. The parent brand surface has a point of view. The light atelier theme, amber accent, Bricolage/Figtree/Spectral pairing, and real team photography feel more distinctive than most studio sites.
2. The work and showcase layer can be genuinely persuasive. The Roastery, MedQueue, school, and fitness screens show range, and MedQueue in particular behaves like a real product.
3. The contact page is calm and practical. Labels, next-step copy, and the 24-hour reply promise reduce anxiety.

## Cognitive Load

Failure count: 4/8, moderate to high.

Failed items:
- Single focus: hero headline, two CTAs, theme toggle, moving project band, and delayed mockup proof compete early.
- Minimal choices: six services, five work items, multiple FAQ categories, and a seven-field inquiry form create equal-weight decisions.
- Chunking: services are grouped by agency taxonomy rather than buyer situations.
- Progressive disclosure: real testimonials, concept bands, case studies, and live demos are not clearly separated.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Proof honesty and naming mismatch**
Why it matters: PRODUCT.md says the concept projects are MedQueue, The Roastery, Aurelia Grand, Greenfield Academy, and Vortex Fitness, and that they must be presented honestly as self-initiated demonstrations. The homepage band instead shows Arya Bakers, Kshetra Yoga Studio, Dr. Meghna's Clinic, Pranav Motors, and others. School case study says Greenfield Academy while the live route says Wellington. Fitness case study says Vortex Fitness while the live route says Ironclad.
Fix: Standardize names across `src/content/work/*`, `src/content/testimonials.ts`, `/work`, and `/showcase/*`. Add explicit labels such as "studio concept" or "craft demo." Remove or qualify simulated testimonials, stats, and client-like outcomes.
Suggested command: `impeccable clarify`

**[P1] Hero does not prove the product fast enough**
Why it matters: DESIGN.md requires a high-quality product mockup showing live showcase work in the hero. On desktop the mockup has too much empty-space competition; on mobile it appears after the copy/CTAs. The first impression still leans on claims before proof.
Fix: Make a real website/device mockup the dominant first-viewport proof. Use screenshots from the strongest showcase, not an abstract planning-board image. Keep one clear CTA pair.
Suggested command: `impeccable layout`

**[P1] Showcase routes undercut their case-study promises**
Why it matters: The sub-websites are supposed to feel launchable and concept-specific. `/showcase/school` is Wellington rather than Greenfield and has broken remote imagery. `/showcase/fitness` is Ironclad and uses "No excuses," while the Vortex case study explicitly critiques that trope.
Fix: Either rename the case studies to match the actual demos or rebuild the demos to match the stated concepts. Fix broken images and contrast risks before judging the concepts finished.
Suggested command: `impeccable harden`

**[P1] Performance has drifted below the stated target band**
Why it matters: Current production Lighthouse checks were home 86, work 88, contact 89 for performance, with LCP around 3.7s-4.1s. Accessibility, best practices, and SEO remain strong at 96/100/100. The prior Kivox target was performance 90+.
Fix: Re-profile LCP on home/work/contact after preserving brand fonts. Likely candidates are hero image weight, carousel/mockup rendering, lazy section placeholder behavior, and font preload balance.
Suggested command: `impeccable optimize`

**[P2] Service taxonomy is still agency-first**
Why it matters: SMB owners think in situations: "new website," "old site hurts trust," "I need bookings/leads," "I need support." The homepage currently asks them to parse Custom Web Development, UI/UX Redesign, SEO & Performance, Branding & Identity, E-Commerce, and Support.
Fix: Collapse the homepage to three buyer paths, then disclose detailed services later: Launch, Improve, Operate/Grow.
Suggested command: `impeccable distill`

**[P2] Light-first contract is technically inconsistent**
Why it matters: DESIGN.md says the site loads light unless the visitor explicitly toggles dark. `themeScript.ts` respects system dark on first visit, so a dark-mode OS user does not get the intended primary experience.
Fix: Default first-time visitors to light; persist dark only after explicit user action.
Suggested command: `impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan, confused first-timer**
- May not know whether the moving name band represents clients, concepts, or examples.
- Encounters broad agency terms before buyer-situation framing.
- Needs the FAQ to explain proof status, but it mostly explains process and pricing.

**Casey, distracted mobile visitor**
- The hero CTA is easy to tap, but proof image comes after the decision.
- Contact form is long for mobile and starts with defaults that may not match intent.
- Fitness/school showcase hero screens are visually strong but dense and sometimes top-heavy.

**Riley, deliberate stress tester**
- Finds naming drift quickly: Greenfield vs Wellington, Vortex vs Ironclad.
- Finds broken school images and a cafe newsletter input without a label.
- Questions client-like stats/testimonials in concept case studies.

**Project-specific: time-poor SMB owner**
- Wants reassurance before booking a call. The site shows craft, but does not always distinguish real testimonial proof from self-initiated concept proof.
- Needs pricing/timeline/process confidence quickly; some of that exists, but it arrives after several high-craft sections.

## Minor Observations

- Repeated bracketed section labels are part of the current design system, but they now read as a little too editorial-scaffolded across the full homepage.
- The homepage proof band should be labeled the way DESIGN.md specifies: "Projects we've crafted."
- The email template uses Inter, which is not fatal, but it is a brand-surface drift.
- The cafe newsletter input should get an accessible label.
- Avoid visible em dashes in content if the design contract continues to ban them.
- Team roles could be more buyer-readable: "AI & App Development" and "R&D, Ui/Ux" feel internal.

## Questions to Consider

1. Should the next pass prioritize trust cleanup, hero proof, or showcase hardening?
2. Are Arya Bakers/Kshetra/Dr. Meghna meant to be real proof, future concepts, or temporary names?
3. Should the showcase demos become fully honest fictional worlds, or should the case studies stop using client-like claims?
4. For mobile SMB visitors, is the primary action "book a call" or "see proof first"?

## Recommended Actions

1. `impeccable clarify`: align proof language, concept labels, testimonials, and naming across the parent site.
2. `impeccable harden`: fix broken showcase images, cafe label, contrast risks, light-first behavior, and demo route mismatches.
3. `impeccable layout`: make the hero proof image more dominant and reduce competing first-fold elements.
4. `impeccable distill`: simplify services into buyer situations and reduce equal-weight decisions.
5. `impeccable optimize`: restore home/work/contact performance to the 90+ band without sacrificing brand fonts.
6. `impeccable polish`: final pass after structural fixes.
